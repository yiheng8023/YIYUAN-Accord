priority specialization inside that domain, not a synonym for the domain.

The existing `STM-01..26`, `P1..24`, and `SG-01..12` coordinates remain useful

`closure-contract` remain candidate interventions. They must not define the

## Two-Level Coverage Model

### General mother framework

Every scenario can be projected across these independent axes:

1. subjects and affected parties;
2. goals, values, intent, and consequential decisions;
3. work and collaboration modes;
4. cognition, competence, learning, accessibility, and agency;
5. context, knowledge, state, memory, and provenance;
6. capabilities, tools, data, authority, actions, and reversibility;
7. evidence, assurance, review, acceptance, and accountability;
8. organization, incentives, rights, costs, and impacts;
9. lifecycle, operations, evolution, recovery, and retirement.

This is an open-world, versioned coverage model. It must expose unknown,
unassessed, contested, and counterexample cells. It cannot close merely because
a fixed inventory is fully mapped.

### Cross-cutting process fidelity and loss

Every axis and domain also carries an end-to-end transformation risk. Goals,
constraints, meanings, evidence, uncertainty, authority, responsibility,
versions, and acceptance obligations may be omitted, distorted, invented, or
amplified when work crosses human, Agent, tool, context, compression,
delegation, handoff, review, and lifecycle boundaries.

This is not a standalone Skill or one extra lifecycle stage. It is a governed
property of the edges between stages. A material transform must make its source
authority, required invariants, input/output identity, omitted constraints,
added assumptions, evidence and authority deltas, hop verification, cumulative
loss budget, downstream blast radius, detection latency, and rollback anchor
inspectable. Local success, unchanged files, or a fresh handoff cannot be
promoted into a lossless end-to-end claim.

### Software-engineering specialization

Software engineering receives a finer lifecycle projection:

1. strategy, product discovery, acquisition, and supply;
2. stakeholder needs and requirements;
3. system and software architecture;
4. detailed design and modeling;
5. implementation, build, and dependency management;
6. review, static quality, and configuration control;
7. verification, validation, testing, and acceptance;
8. security, privacy, safety, and software supply chain;
9. release, delivery, deployment, and change management;
10. operations, observability, reliability, and support;
11. incident response, recovery, and learning;
12. maintenance, evolution, refactoring, and technical debt;
13. migration, deprecation, retirement, and disposal;
14. engineering management, economics, teamwork, and professional practice.

ISO/IEC/IEEE 12207:2026 supplies a current lifecycle reference. SWEBOK V4.0a
supplies a current discipline-knowledge reference. NIST SSDF supplies a
security-development specialization. None is treated as a complete human-AI
collaboration ontology or proof that a local control works.

  registry names broad digital-work domains. It does not prove evidence-backed
  coverage inside every domain.
- `acceptance.full-chain-coverage-matrix` continues to prove that all 62

  It does not prove whole-demand or software-lifecycle completeness.

  for their bound demand lanes and tested environments.
- context, Git, MCP, Skill-exposure, and ablation results remain narrow
  behavior evidence. They do not gain or lose validity merely because the
  problem model is rebaselined.

The new acceptance gate is separate: generalized coverage, positive residual
gap, repository authoring, and future hard-standard extraction must pass
solution neutrality, open-world coverage, software-lifecycle specialization,
and AI-independent hard-standard checks.
